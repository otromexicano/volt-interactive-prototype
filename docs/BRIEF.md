## 1. Product problem

**DESIGN DECISION — WORKING PROBLEM STATEMENT / NOT VALIDATED RESEARCH**

Volt needs an electronics shopping experience that helps people explore and understand products without feeling overwhelmed, while keeping human support easy to access.

**DESIGN DECISION — WORKING HOW MIGHT WE / NOT VALIDATED RESEARCH**

How might we create an approachable electronics shopping experience that makes discovering products feel simple and delightful, while keeping human support easy to reach?

These statements are derived from the source brief and approved design analysis. They remain working design framing until supported by appropriate evidence.

## 2. Target user requirement from the source brief

**FACT — SOURCE BRIEF:** The original Goodbrief names women as the target audience.

**DESIGN DECISION — WORKING:** Volt will not translate that requirement into gender stereotypes. Audience-specific visual or interaction choices require evidence that is not currently available.

**OPEN QUESTION:** Validated audience needs, contexts, accessibility requirements, shopping behavior, and support expectations have not been supplied.

## 3. Working user and job framing

**ASSUMPTION — REQUIRES VALIDATION:** People shopping for everyday electronics may need a clearer, less technically overwhelming way to understand which products fit their needs.

**ASSUMPTION — REQUIRES VALIDATION:** Contextual human support may help when a person is uncertain about product selection.

**Working job framing:** Explore everyday electronics, understand relevant differences without unnecessary technical complexity, and reach human support when clarification is needed.

The working job framing is a synthesis for design use, not a research finding or validated behavior.

## 4. Product context

**FACT — SOURCE BRIEF:** Volt is described as a corner shop selling consumer electronics.

**FACT — SOURCE BRIEF:** The requested experience is an e-commerce website with landing, information, product, privacy-policy, and team/about content.

**FACT — SOURCE BRIEF:** Making it easy to contact the company is the primary stated goal; encouraging catalogue exploration is secondary.

**FACT — SOURCE BRIEF:** The desired perception combines superior quality, affordability, a sense of wonder, and a “home-made feel.” The requested visual direction is trendy, the requested brand color is white, and the generated deadline is one week.

**ASSUMPTION — REQUIRES VALIDATION:** “Home-made feel” may be interpreted as warmth and approachability rather than literal handmade aesthetics.

**ASSUMPTION — REQUIRES VALIDATION:** White may function better as a visual foundation than as the complete identity.

The planned deliverable is an interactive browser-based UI prototype focused on navigation, interaction, responsive behavior, and design-system fidelity—not a production commerce platform.

## 5. Core intended workflow

**DESIGN DECISION — WORKING:** Product discovery should remain central to the shopping experience while support remains easy to access contextually.

The provisional experience flow is:

1. Discover or browse products.
2. Understand options with clear, approachable information.
3. Identify products that may fit the shopper’s needs.
4. Reach contextual human support when clarification is needed.
5. Access the required informational, privacy, and team content.

The final information architecture, catalogue taxonomy, primary flow, and navigation model are not yet defined.

## 6. Key constraints

- Preserve the source distinction between contact as the primary stated goal and catalogue exploration as the secondary stated goal.
- Balance product discovery with accessible human support.
- Communicate quality and affordability without unsupported claims.
- Interpret warmth without relying on literal or stereotyped visual cues.
- Pursue contemporary visual distinction without sacrificing usability, readability, navigation, or accessibility.
- Remain inclusive rather than relying on gender stereotypes.
- Target WCAG 2.2 AA; automated checks will not replace manual review.
- Use a Primitive → Semantic → Component token architecture once foundations are created.
- Preserve approved history, alternatives, QA findings, and remediation records.
- Do not claim research, usability, analytics, business performance, product impact, or production outcomes that have not occurred.

## 7. Edge cases and unresolved conditions currently known

All items below are **OPEN QUESTIONS** unless otherwise labeled:

- Validated user needs and actual shopping behavior.
- Product catalogue structure, taxonomy, product relationships, and specific inventory.
- The reason contact is the primary business goal.
- What kinds of help or contact channels users and the business need.
- Definitions of “home-made feel,” “trendy,” superior quality, and affordability in this context.
- Accessibility needs beyond the project’s WCAG 2.2 AA target.
- Business metrics and success measures.
- Technical constraints, content availability, data shape, and integration requirements.
- Responsive priorities and device-specific behavior.
- Empty, unavailable, incomplete, or changing product information.
- Error, offline, loading, and no-result states.
- Validated usability outcomes.

## 10. Success criteria for this design exercise

Success is directional and quality-based, not a numerical business KPI. The exercise should:

- Make product discovery easy to understand.
- Reduce unnecessary technical complexity.
- Preserve easy access to support.
- Communicate accessible quality and affordability without unsupported claims.
- Feel contemporary without sacrificing usability.
- Remain inclusive rather than relying on gender stereotypes.
- Demonstrate responsive behavior and design-system fidelity in Figma and the browser prototype.
- Meet the project’s WCAG 2.2 AA target through automated and manual review.
- Maintain clear traceability among source facts, assumptions, decisions, open questions, design artifacts, implementation, and QA.

## 11. What this case study must prove

The final case study is intended to demonstrate:

- Product Thinking
- UX Reasoning
- Visual Design
- Design Systems
- Responsive Design
- Accessibility
- Design Engineering
- QA
- AI-assisted production with human quality governance

It must show the reasoning, decisions, iterations, implementation, audits, and verified remediation that actually occur. It must not claim product or business outcomes, fabricated research, or unsupported impact. AI may be documented as an accelerator, but human direction, selection, approval, and quality governance are the story of control.
